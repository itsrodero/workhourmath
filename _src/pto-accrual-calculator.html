The three common ways PTO accrues

  • Per hour worked: for example 1 hour of PTO for every 30 hours worked. Part-time and full-time employees earn at the same rate, in proportion to their hours.
  • Per pay period: a fixed number of hours on each paycheck, such as 4 hours every two weeks (104 hours a year).
  • Per year: a yearly allowance like 15 days, either given up front or spread across the year. Spread out, it accrues at 1/52 per week.
Per hour worked: PTO a week = hours worked a week × earned ÷ per hours Per pay period: PTO a week = hours per period × periods a year ÷ 52 Per year: PTO a week = yearly hours ÷ 52 (days × hours in a day) Balance later = current balance + PTO a week × weeks − hours used (up to the cap)

Worked example: 1 hour of PTO for every 30 hours worked, 40 hours a week

  1. Per week: 40 ÷ 30 = 1.333 hours.
  2. Per biweekly paycheck: 2.67 hours.
  3. Per year: 1.333 × 52 = 69.3 hours, or about 8.7 eight-hour days.

Caps, carryover and "use it or lose it"

Many employers stop accrual once your balance reaches a cap — often one to two times your yearly accrual — so you don't build up an unlimited amount. Some states don't allow "use it or lose it" policies that take away earned vacation, and some require earned vacation to be paid out when you leave; California is the best-known example. Paid sick leave laws in many states and cities set their own minimum accrual, such as 1 hour for every 30 hours worked. Your employee handbook has the rules that apply to you.

Frequently asked questions

How much PTO is 1 hour for every 30 hours worked?

About 69 hours a year at 40 hours a week — just under 9 eight-hour days.

How many hours is 15 days of PTO?

120 hours at 8 hours a day. Spread over the year that's 2.31 hours a week or 4.62 hours per biweekly paycheck.

Do I earn PTO on overtime hours?

If you accrue per hour worked, many policies count every hour worked, including overtime, but some cap accrual at 40 hours a week. Check your policy.
