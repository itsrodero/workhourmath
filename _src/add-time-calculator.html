How to add hours and minutes

Adding time trips people up because minutes carry over at 60, not 100. The reliable way is to add the hours and the minutes separately, then turn every 60 minutes into an hour:

  1. Add the hours: 8 + 7 + 8 = 23 hours.
  2. Add the minutes: 15 + 50 + 5 = 70 minutes.
  3. Carry the minutes: 70 minutes = 1 hour 10 minutes, so the total is 24 hours 10 minutes.
  4. In decimal hours: 24 + 10 ÷ 60 = 24.17 hours.
Total minutes = (hours₁ + hours₂ + …) × 60 + (minutes₁ + minutes₂ + …) Hours and minutes = total minutes ÷ 60 (whole hours) and the remainder (minutes) Decimal hours = total minutes ÷ 60

Don't add times as decimals. 7:50 + 8:15 typed into a regular calculator as 7.50 + 8.15 gives 15.65 — but the right answer is 16 hours 5 minutes, or 16.08 hours.

Subtracting time

Switch a line to − to take time away, for example to subtract unpaid breaks from a total or to see how many hours you have left. When the minutes don't go, borrow an hour: 10:15 − 3:40 becomes 9:75 − 3:40 = 6 hours 35 minutes.

Adding time to a clock time

To find when a shift ends or what time it will be after a delay, add the hours and minutes to the start time and wrap around at midnight. 8:45 AM + 8 hours 30 minutes = 5:15 PM. 10:00 PM + 8 hours = 6:00 AM the next day. In 24-hour time, 12:41 + 0:30 = 13:11, which is 1:11 PM. The clock time tool above does this for you and says when the result falls on the next day.

When totals pass 24 hours

A total of hours worked isn't a clock time, so it can go past 24: 41 hours 30 minutes is a normal weekly total. The calculator also shows it as days, hours and minutes (1 day 17 hours 30 minutes) when that's useful, such as for a total of on-call or travel time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add hours and minutes in Excel?

Enter times as h:mm (for example 7:50), add them with =SUM(), and format the result cell as [h]:mm so it doesn't reset at 24 hours. Multiply by 24 to get decimal hours.

What is 7:45 plus 8:30?

16 hours 15 minutes: 7 + 8 = 15 hours and 45 + 30 = 75 minutes = 1 hour 15 minutes. In decimal, 16.25 hours.

What time is 30 minutes from 12:41?

1:11. Add 30 to 41 minutes = 71 minutes = 1 hour 11 minutes, so 12:41 becomes 1:11 (13:11 in 24-hour time if it's 12:41 PM).

What is 59 minutes plus 15 minutes?

1 hour 14 minutes: 59 + 15 = 74 minutes, and 74 − 60 = 14. In decimal hours that's 1.23.

How do I add minutes and seconds?

The same way, with 60 seconds to a minute: add the minutes and the seconds separately, then turn every 60 seconds into a minute. 2:45 + 1:30 (minutes:seconds) = 3 minutes 75 seconds = 4 minutes 15 seconds.

Can I add more than eight lines?

Add the first eight, note the total, then reset and start the next batch with that total on the first line.
