How double time pay is calculated

Double time means twice your regular rate for each hour it applies to. A $20.00-an-hour job pays $40.00 for a double-time hour, compared with $30.00 for a time-and-a-half hour.

Double time rate = regular rate × 2 Double time pay = double-time hours × regular rate × 2 Gross pay = regular hours × rate + overtime hours × rate × 1.5 + double-time hours × rate × 2

Worked example: $20.00 an hour, 40 regular hours, 8 overtime hours and 4 double-time hours

  1. Regular: 40 × $20.00 = $800.00.
  2. Overtime: 8 × $30.00 = $240.00.
  3. Double time: 4 × $40.00 = $160.00.
  4. Gross pay: $1,200.00 for 52 hours of work.

When is double time required?

  • Federal law doesn't require double time. The Fair Labor Standards Act only requires time and a half for hours over 40 in a workweek. It also doesn't require extra pay for working on holidays, weekends or nights.
  • California requires double time for hours worked over 12 in a workday, and for hours over 8 on the seventh consecutive day of work in a workweek (California Labor Code section 510).
  • Other states: Alaska, Colorado and Nevada have daily overtime, but at time and a half — no state law outside California requires double time in general. See overtime rules by state.
  • Employer policies and union contracts often add double time for holidays, Sundays, a sixth or seventh day, or being called back in. If your contract promises it, that promise is what you're owed.

California double time, day by day

Choose "Hours per day" in the calculator and it applies California's rules for you: hours over 8 in a day are overtime, hours over 12 are double time, regular hours over 40 in the week become overtime, and on a seventh consecutive day of work the first 8 hours are overtime and the rest double time.

Worked example (California): $25.00 an hour; Monday 10 h, Tuesday 13 h, Wednesday 8 h, Thursday 12 h, Friday 9 h

  1. Monday: 8 regular + 2 overtime. Tuesday: 8 regular + 4 overtime + 1 double time. Wednesday: 8 regular. Thursday: 8 regular + 4 overtime. Friday: 8 regular + 1 overtime.
  2. Week: 40 regular, 11 overtime, 1 double time — 52 hours.
  3. Pay: 40 × $25.00 + 11 × $37.50 + 1 × $50.00 = $1,462.50.

Under federal rules only, the same 52 hours would be 40 regular and 12 overtime: $1,450.00. California's daily rules turn the 13th hour on Tuesday into double time.

Frequently asked questions

Is double time 2 times my base pay?

It's 2 times your regular rate. For most hourly workers that's their hourly wage, but if you also earn shift differentials or non-discretionary bonuses, the regular rate — and so your double time — can be a bit higher.

Do I get double time for working on a holiday?

Only if your employer's policy or your contract says so. Federal law doesn't require any extra pay for holidays, and neither does California law on its own — though California's daily and seventh-day rules still apply if your holiday shift triggers them.

When does California pay double time?

For hours over 12 in one workday, and for hours over 8 on the seventh consecutive day worked in a workweek. Some workers, like certain exempt employees and those under special industry orders, follow different rules.

Is double time taxed at a higher rate?

No. Double-time pay is ordinary wages. A bigger paycheck can mean more tax withheld that period, but the hours aren't taxed at a special rate.
