Why use WorkHourMath?

Most pay mistakes start with a small arithmetic slip: adding 7:45 and 8:30 as decimals, forgetting that minutes carry at 60, or counting overtime over two weeks instead of one. Our calculators do the time math in minutes, show both hours and minutes and decimal hours, and apply overtime per workweek, the way federal law requires — plus the daily rules of California, Alaska and Colorado when you choose them.

Every calculator page explains the formula, works through an example you can check by hand and lists its sources, such as the Department of Labor's overtime fact sheet and the rounding rules in federal regulations. Read how we calculate for the details.

Results are estimates for information, not legal or payroll advice. Your employer's policy, a union contract or local law can change the numbers, so if your paycheck doesn't match, start with your payroll department.
