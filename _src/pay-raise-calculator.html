How to calculate a raise

New pay = current pay × (1 + raise % ÷ 100) Raise % = (new pay − current pay) ÷ current pay × 100 Extra per year = (new hourly − old hourly) × 2,080

Worked example: a 4% raise on $20.00 an hour

  1. New rate: $20.00 × 1.04 = $20.80 an hour.
  2. That's $0.80 more an hour, or $0.80 × 2,080 = $1,664 more a year at full time.
  3. Per biweekly paycheck: $1,664 ÷ 26 = $64.00 more, before taxes.

Is my raise keeping up?

A raise smaller than inflation means your pay buys a bit less than before, even though the number went up. To compare, look up the latest 12-month change in the Consumer Price Index from the Bureau of Labor Statistics: if prices rose 3% and your raise was 4%, your real pay rose about 1%. Raises at the same job are often in the low single digits, while changing jobs or moving up a level usually brings bigger jumps — which is why it's worth knowing exactly what an offer is worth per hour and per year.

Raise in dollars or in percent?

The same raise can sound very different. $1 an hour is 6.7% on $15 but 3.3% on $30. Switch the calculator to The raise in $ or My new pay to see the percentage, which is the fairest way to compare two raises or two offers.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 3% raise on $18 an hour?

$18.54 an hour — 54 cents more, or about $1,123 more a year at full time.

What is a 5% raise on a $50,000 salary?

$52,500 a year: $2,500 more, or about $208 more a month before taxes.

What percent raise is $15 to $17 an hour?

13.3% ($2 ÷ $15).
