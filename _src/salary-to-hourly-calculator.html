How to convert a salary to an hourly rate

Divide the yearly salary by the number of hours you work in a year. For a standard full-time job that's 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks).

Hourly rate = yearly salary ÷ (hours a week × weeks a year) Full time = yearly salary ÷ 2,080 Quick check: halve the salary and drop the thousands → $60,000 ≈ $30 an hour

Worked example: a $60,000 salary

  1. At 40 hours a week: $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85 an hour.
  2. At 50 hours a week: $60,000 ÷ 2,600 = $23.08 an hour — the real rate drops when salaried hours go up.
  3. Per paycheck: $60,000 ÷ 26 = $2,307.69 biweekly, or $5,000 a month.

Salaried vs hourly: what changes

Hourly employees are paid for every hour they work, plus overtime over 40 a week if they're non-exempt. Many salaried employees are "exempt" and get the same pay however many hours they work, so working 50 hours instead of 40 lowers their effective hourly rate by 20%. Being paid a salary doesn't automatically make someone exempt, though: the job also has to meet a salary threshold and the duties tests in the federal rules. Read more in how overtime works.

$100,000 a year is how much an hour?

$48.08 an hour full time.

Should I use 2,080 or 2,000 hours?

2,080 (40 × 52) is the standard. 2,000 is a quick estimate that assumes two unpaid weeks off, and it gives a slightly higher hourly rate.
