How overtime pay is calculated

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees must be paid at least one and a half times their regular rate for every hour worked over 40 in a workweek.

Regular hours = hours worked, up to 40 Overtime hours = hours worked − 40 Overtime rate = hourly rate × 1.5 Gross pay = regular hours × rate + overtime hours × overtime rate

Worked example: 46 hours at $20.00 an hour

  1. Regular: 40 × $20.00 = $800.00.
  2. Overtime: 6 hours × $30.00 (1.5 × $20) = $180.00.
  3. Gross pay: $980.00 for the week — $60 more than 46 straight-time hours.

Overtime is per workweek, not per day or pay period

Federal law looks at each workweek on its own: a fixed, recurring block of seven consecutive days that your employer sets. A 10-hour day isn't overtime federally if the week stays at 40, and a 50-hour week isn't cancelled out by a 30-hour week after it. States can add daily overtime: California pays overtime after 8 hours in a day and double time after 12, Alaska after 8, Colorado after 12, and Nevada after 8 for lower-paid workers. See overtime rules by state, or let the time card calculator apply them shift by shift.

Your overtime rate may be higher than 1.5 × your base rate

Overtime is based on your regular rate, which includes most extra pay you earn in the week — shift differentials, production bonuses, commissions — not just your base hourly wage. If you earned $900 straight-time pay (including a $40 night differential) for 45 hours, your regular rate is $900 ÷ 45 = $20.00, and each overtime hour earns an extra half of that: 5 × $10.00 = $50.00. The shift differential calculator shows the effect. Some payments, like discretionary bonuses, gifts and true holiday premiums, are left out.

Who gets overtime?

Most hourly workers are "non-exempt" and get overtime. Some salaried employees in executive, administrative, professional, computer and outside sales jobs are "exempt" if they're paid a salary above a set threshold and their duties meet the tests in the regulations. Being paid a salary alone doesn't make someone exempt. If you're not sure, the Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division can help.

Frequently asked questions

What is time and a half for $15 an hour?

$22.50 an hour ($15 × 1.5).

What is time and a half for $20 an hour?

$30.00 an hour.

What is double time for $18 an hour?

$36.00 an hour ($18 × 2).

Do I get overtime for working on a holiday or weekend?

Not under federal law, unless the hours push you over 40 in the workweek. Many employers pay a holiday or weekend premium anyway, by policy or union contract.

Does paid time off count toward the 40 hours?

Not under federal law: overtime is based on hours actually worked, so vacation, holidays and sick days you're paid for but don't work don't count. Some employers count them anyway.
